# Item 3 – Employee holding statement


Sent at 09:15 by SMS and manager cascade. **Length: 191 words** (199 with the subject line; limit 150–200). Structure follows acknowledge, orient, act (ICDFA, n.d., slide 8): it states the category at a high level, confirms containment is under way, gives workarounds and commits to an exact next time (slide 7).


> **Subject:** Incident notice 1: shared files unavailable (Monday, 09:15)
>
> Dear colleagues,
>
> Since 08:15, many of us cannot open shared files. We know this disrupts your work, and we are sorry.
>
> Our security team is treating this as a suspected cyber incident and has started containment. The cause, the affected systems and any access to information are not yet confirmed. We will not guess.
>
> An image about this incident is circulating in staff chats and outside the organisation. It is unverified. Do not forward, reply to or comment on it, or contact anyone named in it. Tell the Incident Desk where you saw it: 0700 555 0100.
>
> Until we say otherwise: do not use the shared drive; do not restart or switch off your computer unless the security team tells you to; do not plug in USB drives. Your manager will give you a temporary way to work. We will never ask for passwords or bank details by SMS or chat.
>
> Payroll is scheduled for Friday. We cannot yet say whether it is affected. Finance is preparing options; expect a payroll update by 12:00 tomorrow.
>
> Next update to all staff: 12:00 today, by SMS and through your manager.
>
> Northbridge Incident Command


*Table 4. Why the statement is written this way.*


| **Design choice** | **Reason** |
|---|---|
| **“Suspected cyber incident”, not “ransomware”** | The category itself is unconfirmed. A confirmed-sounding word would travel outside faster than the facts. |
| **The image is named but not described** | Describing it would repeat and amplify any claim in it. The statement neither confirms nor denies it, and so does not validate the attacker’s message. |
| **“Do not forward … tell the Incident Desk”** | Stops spread and gives the Technical Lead sightings to trace, without forwarding evidence through untrusted chat. |
| **Payroll: “cannot yet say” plus a 12:00 Tuesday checkpoint** | Gives certainty about timing, not about the outcome, so nothing is promised that may fail (slide 15). |
| **“We will never ask for passwords or bank details”** | Pre-empts payroll fraud that exploits the incident. |
