# Item 7 – Rationale: escalation threshold and what I did not claim


*Word count: 245 (limit 250).*


**Escalation threshold.** I escalate to crisis-level communication at 08:30, not at the four-hour mark, because the slide 13 thresholds are already crossed. Several departments are degraded with no recovery time (duration: high, “no ETA”), payroll is at risk (service: high) and a screenshot is spreading outside the organisation (external exposure: at least medium). Any single high signal escalates (ICDFA, n.d., slide 13), and a crisis begins when technical uncertainty meets external visibility (slide 2). Further triggers go to the Board Chair: likely personal-data access, a media or regulator enquiry, failure of the Tuesday 12:00 payroll gate, or evidence that the screenshot is genuine, which would change what employees must be told.


**What I deliberately did not claim.** I named no attacker, cause or entry point. I did not say data was taken, and I did not say that no data was lost, because early findings are often reversed (slide 7). I gave no recovery time and no promise that payroll will run on Friday. I neither confirmed nor denied the screenshot and described none of its content. I did not say a regulator must be told: Legal decides that, and the planning clock only prevents surprise. I kept “ransomware” out of staff and partner messages, because the category is suspected. Each item has an owner and a check time in the ledger, so a claim moves from unknown to stated only on evidence, never because silence feels uncomfortable or a stakeholder is pressing for reassurance.



# Critical reflection


*Word count: 146 (limit 150).*


The hardest dilemma was payroll. Employees were anxious, and the fastest comfort was to say salaries are safe. I could not say that: payroll depends on file-share data whose integrity is unverified and on a bank cut-off I have not confirmed. A reassurance that fails on Thursday would damage trust far more than an honest unknown today (ICDFA, n.d., slides 3 and 7).


So I traded comfort for usefulness. The employee statement admits what is unconfirmed, then restores agency: clear operating rules, a way to report the screenshot and a firm payroll checkpoint at 12:00 tomorrow. I accepted fifteen minutes of legal review because a premature promise cannot be withdrawn once staff have relied on it. I moved quickly on what is certain, namely acknowledgement, instructions and timing, and deliberately slowly on what is not. Speed applies to what we know; accuracy governs what we claim.
